Master's Thesis Project

Dusty Galaxies in ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey (ALCS)

Repository →

This project uses optical/NIR photometry from Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and Spitzer combined with ALMA 1.2 mm continuum data across 33 galaxy lensing cluster fields. Gravitational lensing by cluster magnification enables the study of faint, dust-obscured star-forming galaxies at high redshift.

1. Multi-band Source Cross-Matching

Automated spatial cross-matching between optical/NIR catalogs and 1.2 mm ALMA detections.

Script: MatchAndPlot.py
2. Modified Blackbody SED Rescaling

Rescaling modified blackbody distributions to ALMA 1.2 mm flux density to derive dust temperature, infrared luminosity, and dust mass.

Script: main_body.py
3. Star Formation Rate ($SFR_{\mathrm{UV+IR}}$)

Deriving total star formation rates from combined unobscured UV and dust-obscured IR luminosities.

Script: SfrCalculate.py
4. Resting UVJ Color Diagrams

Testing resting UVJ color space to distinguish between red quiescent galaxies and dust-obscured starbursts.

Output: Examples-of-Output/

Master's Thesis

The Optical and Infrared Properties of Dusty Star-forming Galaxies in ALCS

University of Copenhagen · Cosmic Dawn Center (DAWN), Niels Bohr Institute · May 2022

"Dust is a powerful proxy for studying star formation and interstellar molecules. In this thesis, we combined ALCS 1.2 mm continuum maps with HST and Spitzer photometric catalogs to study dusty star-bursting candidates at $z = 0 \sim 5$. Results demonstrate that combining UV and IR indicators describes star formation activity far better than SED fitting alone, and UVJ diagrams reliably distinguish quiescent galaxies from dust-reddened star-forming galaxies."
